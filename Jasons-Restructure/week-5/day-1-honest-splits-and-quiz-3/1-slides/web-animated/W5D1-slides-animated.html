Expo in time series comes in three flavours

Three ways a score gets faked

👥

Neighbours in training

Shuffle the hours: the test hour at 10:00 has 09:00 and 11:00 in training. Almost the same reading.

🔮

Features that peek ahead

shift(-1) or rolling(..., center=True) use readings that don't exist yet.

🔑

Settings tuned on the key

Picking the threshold or contamination by looking at the test answers.

All three give a score that won't survive the next real day in the control room.

A side experiment

Meet the copycat 🐱

For each new hour it finds the one most similar hour it has seen and copies that hour's answer: fault or normal.

copycat = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(),
            KNeighborsClassifier(n_neighbors=1))
copycat.fit(X_train, y_train)   # y = 1 inside a fault
copycat.predict(X_test)

It learns from the answer key (supervised). Our forest never sees labels. The copycat is only here to expose expo.

Nearest neighbour is a fault → “fault!”

Why the random split lies

The test hours have twins in training 👥

EV04 lasts 672 hours. Shuffle them and every test hour inside it has its neighbour in training.
The copycat isn't detecting the fault, it's copying the answer from the next seat.

Why skip 1 September?

The embargo: an empty seat between candidates 🪑

Without a gap, a 24-hour feature on the first test hour reads training hours. The test isn't separate any more.

Rule: embargo ≥ your longest feature window. Our longest is 24 hours, so we skip one day.

Where does “normal” come from?

A dirty reference period makes the fault normal 🧽

EV04 hours lit, out of 672

Fit on a year that contains EV04 and the forest learns the hot amplifier as normal.
In production there are no labels: ask operations for a period they believe was quiet, and write it in your report.
