Why sin and cos?

The year is a clock, not a ruler 🕰️

As a plain number, week 52 and week 1 look 51 apart. On the calendar they are next-door neighbours.

sin52 and cos52 are where the week sits on the circle: two columns instead of 52, and the cycle joins up.

woy = df.index.isocalendar().week
f["sin52"] = np.sin(2 * np.pi * woy / 52)
f["cos52"] = np.cos(2 * np.pi * woy / 52)

Why so narrow?

Two reasons, both fixable 🔍

🏠

It marked its own homework

Training misses: sd 2.84. Misses on weeks it never saw: sd 6.99, and on average 4.1 tickets too high.

🌧️➡️☀️

The season switched off

3 Nov: the rains end. Last week was 74, it forecast 70.9, reality was 53 (-17.9). A smooth sin/cos wave can't make a step.

A band measured on easy weeks is confident about the wrong things.

The honest recipe

Measure the misses it made before it knew the answer

Walk forward through the training weeks from 24 Mar: fit, forecast the next week, write down the miss. 32 honest misses.

Band = forecast ± the 90th-percentile miss. No bell-curve guess: just how wrong it actually was.
