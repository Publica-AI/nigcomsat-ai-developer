Write the rules BEFORE you compute anything

Four scoring rules

1

One flag inside = caught

A flag anywhere inside the event's start→end (±1 step) catches it.

2

Many flags = still ONE catch

EV02 flagged on both of its hours is one catch, not two.

3

Outside every event = false alarm

Each one is a page to the duty engineer at 2 a.m. 📟

4

A 233-day event is ONE event

Flag one day of EV05 and you caught it once, not 233 times.

Decide the rules first, so nobody can bend them after seeing the score. 🧑🏾‍⚖️

The two numbers that matter

Recall and precision

🕸️

Recall

Of the real events, how many did we catch?

recall = caught / real_events

Musa: 9 ÷ 10 = 0.90 👍🏾

📟

Precision

When it shouts, how often is something really there?

precision = caught / all_alarms

Musa: 9 ÷ 200 = 0.045 😩

Ade the sleeper: recall 0. Precision? He never shouted, so there's nothing to measure.

Which number leads?

It depends on what a miss costs

🛰️

Recall first

Battery, power, propulsion. A miss can cost the satellite.
NigComSat-1 was lost in 2008 after a solar-array failure. Accept some extra pages.

📟

Precision first

Rain fade, NOC dashboards, anything that pages people all night.
Too many false alarms and engineers stop reading the pager. That's alarm fatigue.

Write down which one leads before you score, and say why. ✍🏾
